In practice

What the work actually changes.

A legal function from zero, built in the order that mattered

Problem

A startup with no legal function, holding significant customer assets in a high-risk market. Everything needed doing at once, and the wrong order would have been costly in a different way each time.

Approach

Sequenced the work by what would let the business earn soonest, risk-adjusted against what would hurt most if missed. Customer terms first, because they unlocked revenue; then policies, compliance and IP — each call informed by how that industry actually fails.

Result

Revenue started early and safely. Liability was capped tightly enough to deter claims, compliance could be demonstrated when the regulator came knocking, and every piece of IP stayed with the business. It was later acquired.

Product terms that stopped costing deals

Problem

Standard terms grown organically over years — inconsistent, and no longer matching what the business delivered. Customers wouldn’t sign without amendments, so every deal became its own negotiation.

Approach

Rewrote them to be signed first time: reflecting the services as actually delivered, protecting the business properly, and pitched so a reasonable customer could sign without involving their own lawyers.

Result

Amendment requests largely disappeared and deals closed faster — winning business from competitors whose paperwork still triggered a legal review every time.

6 weeks to 2average time to signature
80% to 10%of deals needing amendments

Getting the regulator to say yes once, not every time

Problem

A demanding regulator required specific outcomes in every supplier contract — and gave inconsistent feedback, with different reviewers taking different views on materially the same terms. Every deal was an unpredictable approval.

Approach

Drafted a standardised set of regulatory provisions and had the regulator approve them in principle. They then became both the fixed basis for every supplier contract and a lever in the negotiation: accept these and the deal clears quickly.

Result

Suppliers had a commercial reason to agree rather than argue. Approval stopped being the bottleneck on growth.

Example catch — the cap that doesn't cap

"Each party's total liability shall not exceed the Fees paid in the preceding 12 months, for claims under this Agreement or under any indemnity given by that party."

The words in gold are what was missing. Without them, the cap doesn't touch indemnity claims.

AI drafts routinely leave indemnity clauses outside the liability cap without saying so. A cap that looks watertight can still leave you exposed to unlimited liability through an indemnity.

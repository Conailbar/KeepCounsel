UK GDPR for early-stage startups: the five things to get right first

Placeholder article for design purposes — first published on LinkedIn.

Most early-stage teams know they need "a privacy policy" and stop there. UK GDPR compliance is genuinely lighter-touch pre-scale than people assume — but a handful of things are worth getting right from day one, before they're expensive to fix.

1. Know what data you actually hold

Not a formal audit — just an honest list: what personal data you collect, where it lives, and why. Most early compliance problems come from not knowing the answer to this, not from getting the legal analysis wrong.

2. Pick a lawful basis for each use, and mean it

"Consent" is not the default answer for everything — for a lot of ordinary product functionality, "legitimate interests" or "contract" is the right basis, and is more robust than a consent banner nobody reads.

3. Get your processor agreements in place

If you use any third-party tool that touches user data (analytics, email, hosting, support), you need a data processing agreement with them — most providers have a standard one; the job is making sure you've actually signed it, not drafting from scratch.

4. Write a privacy policy that matches reality

A privacy policy copied from a template company that does something different from you is worse than no policy — it creates a written record of a promise you're not keeping. Match it to what you actually do.

5. Have a plan for data subject requests

Someone will eventually ask you to delete or export their data. You don't need automated tooling on day one — you need a simple, written process for finding and handling that request within the statutory timeframe.

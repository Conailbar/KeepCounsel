Seven clauses every SaaS terms of service needs before your first enterprise customer

First published on LinkedIn, archived here for reference.

Most early-stage SaaS terms of service are written once, in a hurry, before the first paying customer signs up — and then never revisited until an enterprise procurement team sends back a twelve-page redline. Here's what tends to be missing.

1. A clear, narrow limitation of liability

Vague or missing liability caps are the single most common gap. A well-drafted cap (typically tied to fees paid in a defined period) protects the business without looking evasive to sophisticated counterparties.

2. Data processing terms that actually match your architecture

Generic "GDPR-compliant" boilerplate rarely reflects sub-processors, data location, or retention in practice. Enterprise buyers will ask — have an accurate answer ready before they do.

3. Uptime and service-level commitments you can actually meet

If you don't offer an SLA, say so clearly rather than leaving it ambiguous. If you do, make sure engineering has signed off on the numbers.

4. A workable termination and data-return process

What happens to a customer's data on termination — and within what timeframe — should be explicit, not implied.

5. IP ownership of customer-generated content

Especially relevant for platforms and tools that let users create, upload or generate content — ambiguity here creates real commercial risk later.

6. A sensible change-of-terms mechanism

You will need to update your terms eventually. Build in a fair, clearly signposted process now rather than retrofitting one under time pressure.

7. Governing law and dispute resolution that suits your customer base

The right choice depends on where your customers and company are based — this is rarely a "just copy a template" decision.

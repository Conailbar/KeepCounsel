The legal timeline for a pre-seed SAFE round, week by week

Placeholder article for design purposes.

Legal work is rarely the reason a pre-seed round takes too long — but it becomes the bottleneck when it's left until the last minute. Here's a realistic week-by-week shape.

Week 1 — Instrument & terms

Agree the instrument (SAFE, convertible loan note, or priced round), valuation cap or discount, and any side letters with lead investors. This is a commercial conversation as much as a legal one.

Week 2 — Drafting & internal approvals

First drafts circulate. In parallel, board and shareholder approvals are prepared so signing isn't held up by a missing resolution.

Week 3 — Investor review & negotiation

Investor-side counsel (if any) reviews and comments. Most pre-seed rounds see light negotiation if the instrument is market-standard.

Week 4 — Signing & closing

Signatures collected, funds move, and the cap table, share register (or equivalent) and Companies House filings are updated to reflect the round.

What actually causes delay

In practice, the biggest delays come from unclear cap tables, missing board minutes from earlier decisions, and non-standard side letter requests introduced late — not the core documentation itself.
